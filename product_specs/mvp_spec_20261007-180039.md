## 1. Business problem

The product helps product teams at an IT / tech / consulting company to:

1. Identify duplicate or related requests
2. Understand the customer problem behind each request
3. Separate popular requests from strategically valuable ones
4. Prioritize requests consistently
5. Keep stakeholders informed about decisions and progress
6. Spend less time manually reviewing, grouping and summarizing feedback

The product must solve these with an **AI-first workflow**, not a traditional feature-request board with AI features added on top.

## 2. Core concepts

- **Feature Request**: a raw signal from one client, kept verbatim as evidence.
- **Customer Need**: the product-level problem inferred from one or more related requests. It is the main object PMs analyze, prioritize and decide on. It explains **why** something matters.
- **Problem statement format**: *[Segment] needs to [job] because [reason]; today they [workaround].*
  For example, "Export to Excel", "CSV downloads" and "Google Sheets reporting" all map to *"Clients need to use product data outside the platform."*
- **Project**: a client engagement (e.g. *Reporting Modernization* for Northwind Logistics) with a team. Structure: **Client → Project → Ticket**.
- **Ticket**: a unit of delivery work inside a project — **what** Engineering is building. Every ticket links to exactly one Customer Need; a Need can have tickets in several projects.

Feature Request → Customer Need is the product-intelligence layer. Client → Project → Ticket is the delivery layer. They meet only through the Ticket → Customer Need link.

**Core workflow:** Feature Request → AI understanding → Customer Need → PM decision → Delivery → Stakeholder update

## 3. Roles

The platform serves an IT / consulting firm. Individual Contributors are mainly technical engineers who work directly with clients.

| Role | Who | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Workspace Admin** | Internal leadership | Onboards clients, imports client data, staffs engineers to engagements, owns strategic goals. Read-only on everything else; no triage or roadmap rights. |
| **Product Manager** | Internal (may be titled Product Owner or Practice Lead; one title must be used everywhere) | Triages, edits Needs, scores the rubric, decides, owns tickets, approves every outbound update. |
| **Individual Contributor** | Internal engineers, testers, business analysts | Submits on behalf of staffed clients, adds technical input, works assigned tickets, forwards only approved updates. |
| **Client user** | External client stakeholders | Submits requests, supports existing Needs, follows status and updates. |
| **Prospects** | External, no portal access in P0 | Their needs enter through on-behalf submissions by presales engineers and are tracked as prospect accounts. |

### 3.2 Visibility rules

- **Client confidentiality.** An engineer sees client names, raw requests and context only for clients they are staffed on. For other clients they see aggregate counts with no names.
- **Commercial data.** Contract value / ARR is visible only to the Admin and the PM. Engineers see client tier and segment.
- **Public vs internal fields of a Need.** Client users see only the public fields: problem statement, status, public rationale and supporter count. They never see other clients, contract values, rubric scores, internal notes or engineers' technical notes.
- **Attachments** can be opened by their owner, the PM, and engineers staffed on the request's account; never by other clients. The client Assistant sees only the customer's own requests and files, public Need fields and approved updates.
- **On-behalf submissions** are marked as such and count toward the **client's** demand, never the engineer's.
- **Projects and tickets** are internal. Client users never see tickets, project teams or technical notes; they follow the Customer Need.

## 4. P0 end-to-end workflow

1. **Submit request.** A client user gives any mix of a short description, details and files; AI reviews each file, then shows what it understood (goal, workaround, impact, terms) and the customer confirms or corrects it. An engineer on a client's behalf (choosing client → project) enters a title and the "why", with one AI follow-up when needed. This never creates a ticket. For on-behalf submissions, the AI always asks one follow-up: "What is the client trying to do, and why?"
2. **Detect related.** The request is embedded and compared against existing **Customer Needs** (top 5). The LLM classifies the result as *same / related / new*, with a confidence score and a reason. This runs in real time, during submission.
3. **Match the Need.** The submitter sees "Is this your need?" with the problem statement and status.
   - **Yes:** support the existing Need. The request and its "why" are attached as evidence.
   - **No / no match:** submit as new. The AI drafts a new Need for PM review.
4. **Consolidate evidence (background).** The request is linked to the Need and the AI refines the problem statement. Routing by confidence:
   - User-confirmed or high-confidence matches are attached automatically.
   - Low-confidence matches and proposed new Needs go to the **Triage Inbox**.
5. **PM reviews.** Triage actions are Accept, Move, New Need, Merge, Split and Edit statement, and PM corrections update the Need. The Need detail page shows:
   - the problem statement;
   - an AI brief that cites request IDs;
   - every request, verbatim;
   - Demand and Strategic panels side by side.
6. **AI provides prioritization context.** The AI pre-fills the rubric (section 6), citing evidence for each score, and suggests a priority tier. Effort comes from engineers' estimates on the Need's tickets.
7. **PM decides.** The PM sets the final rubric values, the priority, the status and a **required** rationale. The outcomes are Plan, Defer, Need more info or Not planned.
8. **Communicate.** On every status change, the AI drafts an update and the PM edits and approves it. It is sent by email to requesters, supporters and the engineers staffed on affected clients, and is shown on the Need's timeline.
9. **Deliver.** The PM creates tickets in the relevant projects, links them to the Need and moves them to Planned. Engineers move their assigned tickets to In Development and Released. Each move triggers an AI-drafted update for step 8. From any ticket an engineer can answer *"Why are we building this?"*: Ticket → Customer Need → evidence.

## 5. Signals: popularity vs strategic value

These are two separate signals and are **never blended into one score**.

| Demand (popularity) | Strategic value |
|---|---|
| Number of linked requests | Client tier / segment |
| Unique supporters | Contract value / ARR affected (Admin and PM only) |
| Unique client accounts | Enterprise or strategic clients affected |
| 30- vs 90-day request count (trend) | Fit with strategic goals (AI explains the fit and cites the goals) |

- Demand metrics are computed with SQL when the page loads. There is no separate enrichment pipeline.
- Strategic goals (3–5) are set by the Admin.

## 6. Prioritization rubric

- The same criteria are applied to every Need, each scored 1–5.
- The AI pre-fills every criterion with cited evidence.
- The PM sets the final value and the rationale.

| Criterion | Source |
|---|---|
| Reach | Demand signals |
| Revenue impact | Contract value affected |
| Strategic fit | Strategic goals |
| Severity | Problem statements and the clients' "why" |
| Effort | Engineer estimate; PM sets the final value |

Both the AI's values and the PM's final values are stored. Consistency comes from using a fixed rubric, not from trusting the model.

## 7. Statuses and the delivery workspace

- **Customer Need statuses (public, fixed):** Under Review → Planned → In Development → Released, plus **Not Planned** (requires a public rationale).
- **Ticket statuses (internal, configurable per project):** the PM edits each project's list. Every status has a stage:

  | Stage | Meaning | Rules |
  |---|---|---|
  | Backlog | Not yet agreed | New tickets start in the project's first Backlog status. Only the PM moves tickets out of Backlog. |
  | Planned | Agreed and scheduled | Engineers can pick these up. |
  | In progress | Being built / reviewed / tested | The first linked ticket reaching this stage moves the Customer Need from Planned to In Development. |
  | Done | Finished | When every linked ticket is Done, the Customer Need moves to Released. |

  - Default workflow for new projects: Backlog → Planned → In Development → Released.
  - A workflow must keep at least one Backlog and one Done status, unique names, and stages in order (Backlog, Planned, In progress, Done). Statuses reorder within their stage.
  - Removing a status that tickets use requires choosing a replacement status for those tickets.
  - Engineers move their assigned tickets between any non-Backlog statuses; the PM can move any ticket to any status. Each Need status change drafts a customer update for the PM to approve.
- **Engineer workspace:**
  - **My Work:** Board and Backlog table sharing search plus Project, Client, Priority and Assignee filters. With one project selected the board shows that project's statuses; otherwise the four stages, with each card's own status name.
  - **Projects**, **Project detail**, **Ticket detail** (status shown with the project's full flow) as in v0.2.
- **PM:** Tickets shows delivery tickets across projects (by stage, or one project's own columns); **Projects** lists each project's workflow and opens its status editor; each Customer Need lists its linked tickets.
- **Not in scope:** epics, sub-tasks, sprints, Gantt charts, time tracking, velocity, automations, per-transition rules.

## 9. Architecture (P0)

- One web app.
- Postgres with pgvector.
- One LLM API.
- The AI calls for matching run synchronously at submission time. Other AI work runs in-process after submission.
- Metrics are computed with SQL queries.
- Not used in P0: a dedicated vector DB, queues, an event bus, agent frameworks, multiple models or batch clustering jobs.

## 10. Out of scope for P0

- Public board for browsing and voting
- Intake integrations (Zendesk, Intercom, Slack)
- CRM sync
- Jira / Azure DevOps sync (later: one-way export per engagement)
- Batch re-clustering
- Fully automatic merging without review
- Roadmap views
- Custom scoring frameworks
- SSO and multi-tenancy
- Analytics dashboards
- Prospect portal access
- Defect tracking (defects are tagged and excluded from demand counts)
- Epics, sub-tasks, sprints, Gantt charts, time tracking, velocity metrics, workflow automation

## 11. P0 success criteria

Measured on a seeded dataset of about 200 realistic requests from about 30 client accounts:

- AI grouping matches the PM's grouping for at least 80% of requests.
- "Export to Excel", "CSV downloads" and "Google Sheets reporting" land in one Need about using product data outside the platform.
- The PM never searches, counts or summarizes by hand.
- The top Need by demand and the top Need by strategic value can be different Needs.
- A status change reaches every requester, supporter and staffed engineer, with its rationale.
- No engineer or client user can see data from a client they are not entitled to.
